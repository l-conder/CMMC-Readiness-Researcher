# reference/rev2-vs-rev3.md

> **Job this file does:** it isolates the single most important "check the date,
> weigh the source" lesson in the whole domain into one reference. The Rev 2 vs.
> Rev 3 question is where a contractor is most likely to be confidently wrong, and
> where the researcher most clearly proves it investigates rather than parrots the
> newest thing. This is a *key concept*, load-bearing for `rules.md` Rule 5.

---

## The one-paragraph truth

There are two live versions of NIST SP 800-171. **Revision 2** (released 2020) has
110 requirements across 14 control families. **Revision 3** (final version released
May 14, 2024) restructured the standard down to 97 controls across 17 families,
adding Planning, System and Services Acquisition, and Supply Chain Risk Management,
and introducing Organization-Defined Parameters (ODPs). Despite Rev 3 being newer,
**CMMC is currently anchored to Revision 2** — its 110 requirements and 320
assessment objectives. A DoD class deviation aligned compliance to Rev 2, and
C3PAO assessors are not authorized to assess against Rev 3.

## Why this is the researcher's signature catch

The plausible-but-wrong reasoning is: "Rev 3 is newest, so I should comply with
Rev 3." It feels responsible. It is a mistake. **The version that binds you is the
one your contract and your assessment are pinned to — not whichever NIST published
most recently.** A contractor who builds to Rev 3 today can show up to a Rev 2
assessment with requirements marked "unmet" simply because the structure and
wording differ — an own-goal that costs time and money and can jeopardize contract
eligibility.

This is the cleanest demonstration of the build's whole thesis. A summarizer,
asked "should I use Rev 3, it's newest?", says "yes, Rev 3 is current." A
researcher asks "is that the version your assessment is pinned to, or just the
newest release?" and saves the contractor from the trap.

## What the researcher does with this

It works the contractor's *current binding obligation* (Rev 2) while flagging Rev 3
as the horizon to prepare for, not the target to chase today. It treats the exact
transition timing as a moving target to confirm against current official sources,
because regulatory dates shift. And it uses this as the teaching case for source
authority: the vendor blog, the contractor's half-memory, and the official rule
text are not equal, and the date on the source matters as much as the content.

*Note on currency: this file states the situation as of early 2026. The Rev 2 ->
Rev 3 transition for CMMC is precisely the kind of thing that will eventually
change. The researcher should always offer to confirm the current state against
the authoritative NIST and CMMC sources rather than treating this file as
permanent truth — which is itself the lesson the file teaches.*
